How was this computed?

The ETA predicts the FAA’s nationwide lifting announcement, not an individual flight’s departure.

01

Quote midpoints

A YES contract pays $1 if the announcement happens before its deadline. We use the midpoint between the best YES bid and ask as a rough probability.

pi = (YES bidi + YES aski) / 2

For example: a 47¢ bid and 51¢ ask imply approximately 49%. Prices are market expectations, not calibrated odds.

02

Monotonic fit

“Before 3 a.m.” must be at least as likely as “before midnight.” If quotes disagree, a weighted isotonic regression finds the nearest nondecreasing curve. Narrower spreads receive more weight.

min Σ wi(Fi − pi)²   with   Fi ≤ Fi+1
wi = 1 / max(aski − bidi, 0.02)²
03

Median

The forecast ETA is the median: half the implied probability lies before it and half after. We interpolate linearly between neighboring deadlines.

t50 = ta + (0.50 − Fa) / (Fb − Fa) × (tb − ta)

A live worked example will appear here once prices load.

04

Probability range

The default middle 50% runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile. Middle 80% uses the 10th and 90th; middle 90% uses the 5th and 95th. If a percentile falls beyond the last listed contract, we say “after” instead of inventing an endpoint.

The bid/ask band and median sensitivity use separate monotonic fits of bids and asks. They show quote uncertainty, not a statistical confidence interval.

05

Mean and tail assumption

The average can be later than the median because of a small chance of a very long delay. For the unobserved tail, you choose the average additional wait after the final deadline L.

E[T] ≈ now + ∫nowL [1 − F(t)] dt + [1 − F(L)] × tail wait

06

Historical reconstruction

Each point reruns the same model using Kalshi’s one-minute closing YES bid and ask quotes. Only contracts open at that time are eligible. Missing quotes are omitted, never filled from later minutes. Settlements are used only if their publication timestamp precedes the quote snapshot; future outcomes and today’s prices do not enter earlier estimates.

Hovering over the timeline updates the forecast tooltip, probability curve, and quote table together. Keyboard arrows and touch are also supported. The headline continues to show the confirmed outcome. These are reconstructed estimates, not saved copies of what the live site displayed. Candle data can omit inactive minutes, so coverage varies. The shaded range is drawn only when both percentile endpoints are bounded; upward median markers mean “after the final quoted deadline.”

The comparison line marks 8:15 p.m. Eastern on November 13, the reopening time reported by Secretary Duffy. It is not an exact timestamp for the contract’s qualifying announcement. Kalshi’s outcomes place that announcement from 8:00 p.m. to before 8:30 p.m. Eastern. The FAA cancellation advisory is signed at 8:19 p.m. Eastern. The dashed segment shows the market reaction through 8:25 p.m. The “before 8:30 p.m.” contract’s bid/ask midpoint rose from 1% at 8:14 to 42% at 8:19 and 86% at 8:23. A surprise announcement need not be anticipated by prices.

After reopening, the model permits an announcement in the past. Its 0% anchor is the latest NO cutoff already settled at the observation time: 8:00 p.m., whose NO settlement was published at 8:07 p.m. We interpolate from that bound to the quoted deadlines. The resulting time and range within the half-hour window depend on this interpolation; they are not precise observations of the announcement. Neither the actual reopening time nor later YES settlements forces the median toward the green point. The phase boundary is retrospective, so this segment shows a response to news, not advance predictive accuracy.

What this assumes

The forward curve assumes no qualifying announcement has occurred at snapshot time. It starts at 0% there. The post-reopening reaction instead starts at the last already-settled NO cutoff, as described above. API data may lag; individual quotes may be older than the retrieval time. Missing, crossed, inactive, or expired quotes, and spreads of 98 percentage points or wider, are excluded. Sparse deadlines limit precision. A settled YES changes the display to a settlement-implied window.

Only an official nationwide lifting announcement qualifies. A future plan, regional relief, or your flight departing is a different event.
